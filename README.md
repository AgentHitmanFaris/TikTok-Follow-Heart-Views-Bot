Tabular breakdown of musical timing concepts, operations, and MIDI parameters.
  
  | Concept | Plain English Meaning | Real-World Example | MIDI Parameter | What Happens If Missing / Wrong |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | **Physical Time vs Musical Time** | Physical time is real-world seconds on a stopwatch. Musical time is counting beats and bars in a song. | An audio note starts at `1.25 seconds`, but in music it is `Bar 1, Beat 2`. | Timestamp (seconds) converted to MIDI Ticks (`PPQ`). | Notes play at random seconds with no musical grid or measure lines. |
  | **Tempo (BPM)** | The speed of the musical pulse (Beats Per Minute). Higher = faster, Lower = slower. | `120 BPM` means exactly two beats per second. `60 BPM` means one beat per second. | `set_tempo` (Microseconds per Quarter Note: `500,000` for 120 BPM). | Software defaults to 120 BPM; audio playback drifts out of sync with other instruments. |
  | **Time Signature (Meter)** | Tells you how many beats fit inside one bar (measure), and what kind of note counts as one beat. | `4/4`: 4 quarter beats per bar.<br>`3/4`: 3 quarter beats (waltz).<br>`6/8`: 6 eighth beats per bar. | `time_signature` (Numerator: `4`, Denominator: `4`). | DAWs cannot draw vertical bar lines; sheet music has no measure bars and becomes unreadable. |
  | **Barlines & Measures** | The vertical dividers that slice music into equal blocks so humans can read it. | In `4/4` at 480 ticks/beat: every bar is exactly `1,920 ticks` wide. | Automatic grid calculation from `time_signature` and `ticks_per_beat`. | Notes spill across screens without measure numbers, making rehearsals or score reading impossible. |
  | **Downbeat (First Beat)** | The very first heavy beat (`Beat 1, Bar 1`) of the song. | A song might have 1.5 seconds of silence or a count-in before the music actually starts. | `first_downbeat` offset (shifts all ticks backwards). | Bar lines fall on off-beats; the strong beat lands on Beat 3 or Beat 4 instead of Beat 1. |
  | **Quantization** | Snapping slightly early or late notes onto the nearest perfect rhythm grid. | A human plays a note 0.02 seconds late; quantization snaps it exactly onto the 16th-note grid. | Snapped Tick = `round(Tick / Grid_Ticks) * Grid_Ticks`. | Notes look messy with micro-rests (64th rests) and jitter instead of clean sheet music. |
  | **No Tuplets (Straight Grid)** | Forcing notes to only land on standard halves, quarters, 8ths, or 16ths—never triplets. | `Grid = 4` divides 1 beat into 4 clean parts (16th notes: ticks `0, 120, 240, 360, 480`). | Binary grid division (`1, 2, 4, 8, 16`) prohibiting factor-3 math. | Notation programs inject chaotic triplets, septuplets, and weird tuplet brackets across measures. |
  | **Tuplets (Triplets)** | Squeezing 3 equal notes into the space normally occupied by 2 notes. | Eighth triplets in blues, swing, or 12/8 shuffle: ticks `0, 160, 320, 480`. | Ternary grid division (`3, 6, 12`). | Straight pop/rock songs accidentally turn into swung triplets if tuplet prevention is not locked. |
  | **Gap Filling** | Stretching notes across microscopic pauses so notation looks clean. | A pianist lifts a finger for a split-second between chords; gap filling holds the chord clean. | `fill_gaps_beats` (e.g. holds across gaps $\le 0.25$ beats). | Score notation clutters the page with tiny 32nd-note rests between continuous notes. |
